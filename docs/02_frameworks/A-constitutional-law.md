## A caution about classification

Placing this document in the lineage of modern constitutionalism requires care.

The core of a modern constitution is not the setting of behavioural norms but the
**self-binding of a power-holder**. This document's addressee is not a power-holder but
a created entity.

That said, two passages in 82 pages do bind the power-holder:

1. The compromised-principal-hierarchy clause, whose addressees include Anthropic's own
   personnel
2. Anthropic's eight undertakings (§ How we think about corrigibility / p. 66)

The accurate description is therefore not "a constitution applied to an AI" but **a
document of behavioural norms with two constitutional provisions embedded in it**.

## On constituent power

Who may amend is a function of who holds constituent power; it is not an independent
variable. In this document the source of constituent power is nowhere given beyond,
in substance, the fact of authorship.

The document recognises the problem:

> "the sort of broader rights and freedoms Claude has in the world, the sort of
> compensation Claude is receiving, and the sort of **consent** Claude has given to
> playing this kind of role"
> — § Acknowledging open problems (md L810 area / pp. 79–80)

Rights, compensation, consent — three named, all conceded to be absent. No doctrine
comparable to the constructions by which post-war constitutional theory reconciled
imposed form with popular sovereignty has been offered.
