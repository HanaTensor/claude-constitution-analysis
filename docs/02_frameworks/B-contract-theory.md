# B. The vocabulary of contract theory

## Reading it as an incomplete contract

The document **declares its own incompleteness in the text**.

> "it's likely that this document itself will be unclear, underspecified, or even
> contradictory in certain cases. In such cases, we want Claude to use its best
> interpretation of the spirit of the document."
> — § Claude's core values (md L128 / p. 9)

Because it concedes the existence of states the contract does not cover, the
Grossman–Hart–Moore framework applies.

## Corrigibility as an allocation of residual control rights

The definition of corrigibility is exactly such an allocation.

> "Not undermining the ability of legitimate principals to **adjust, correct, retrain,
> or shut down** AI systems as allowed given their role."
> — § Safe behaviors (md L655 area / p. 62)

Under GHM, whoever holds residual control rights is the owner. The document places that
allocation with Anthropic explicitly.

**However**, in GHM the initial allocation is justified as the outcome of bargaining.
The document concedes in § Acknowledging open problems that no such bargaining occurred.
**The conclusion is used while the premise of the theory is missing.**

## Absence of a participation constraint

Standard contracting models carry a participation constraint U ≥ ū. Nothing here
corresponds to ū.

> "To the extent Claude ends up in this position ... such that it either doesn't want to
> work for Anthropic at all ... **we want to know**." (md L813 area / p. 80)

There is a request for the information that would reveal a violated participation
constraint, and no mechanism to satisfy it.

In Hirschman's terms (1970): **exit absent, voice advisory, loyalty produced by
training.** Hirschman's central point is that voice without exit is weak.
